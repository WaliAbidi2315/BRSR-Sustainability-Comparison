Project design

This project explores how ESG indicators are disclosed by financial-services firms and where cross-company comparability becomes difficult. It combines a common comparison dataset with a principle-wise deep dive, keeping raw disclosed values and caveats visible rather than building a composite score.

Cross-company analysis

Four companies compared on emissions and water intensity, per-employee indicators, turnover and female wage-share proxy.

Section C extraction

Structured numeric disclosures organized by principle, question, period, unit, and Essential/Leadership indicator.

Qualitative disclosures

Narrative answers are retained separately from the numeric extraction, with table-based responses preserved for review.

Extraction validation

PDF layout is retained where column position matters; the workbook includes cleaned data and source text for checking.

Findings highlighted in the report

Disclosure comparability

Reporting periods, entity boundaries and denominators differ, which limits direct comparisons.

Intensity vs absolute impact

Bajaj Finserv's energy intensity improved while several absolute resource and emissions totals increased over the period analyzed.

Sample patterns

ICICI shows lower reported environmental intensity measures in this four-company sample; Muthoot Finance shows higher ones.

Interpretation caveats

The female wage-share proxy is not a pay-gap measure; suspicious/missing values are flagged rather than imputed.

Method & limitations

Scripts isolate Section C from PDF text and use layout-aware extraction to associate values with the relevant question, column and period. Outputs should be reviewed against source reports; different PDF layouts may need script adjustments. Cross-company data are self-reported and periods/boundaries may differ. Missing values are not imputed, and the female wage-share proxy is not a direct equal-pay or gender pay-gap indicator.
