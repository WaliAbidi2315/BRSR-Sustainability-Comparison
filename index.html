Project objective

Compare sustainability disclosures with emphasis on environmental intensity, resource use and workforce indicators, and test how well BRSR makes financial-sector ESG performance comparable. The analysis is descriptive and does not claim to measure each company's overall sustainability performance.

Environmental indicators

GHG emissions and water intensity relative to revenue, including per-employee measures.

Workforce indicators

Employee scale, turnover and the female wage-share proxy as listed in the worksheet.

Principle-wise deep dive

Bajaj Finserv Section C across all nine principles, FY2024 against FY2023, with every value kept as reported.

Disclosure quality

A completeness check showing why a 100% disclosure score can still hide narrative or not-applicable answers.

Key findings

Size does not predict performance

ICICI leads the sample on emissions intensity, water intensity, turnover and female wage share.

Branch-heavy models carry more

Muthoot Finance has the highest emissions and water intensity in the sample.

Growth outpaces intensity gains

Bajaj Finserv's energy intensity improved even as absolute energy, water and Scope 3 emissions grew.

Gender and value-chain gaps

Female wage share ranges from 6.48% to 26%, and Scope 3 and value-chain assessments remain mostly narrative.

Limitations

Reporting periods and boundaries differ across companies, and most figures are self-reported and unaudited. Some cells are blank and are not estimated. Bajaj Finserv's water per employee looks like a unit error. The female wage-share proxy is not a gender pay-gap or equal-pay measure. The report's deep-dive sections are headed "Bajaj Finance", but the figures match Bajaj Finserv (BFS Group). Validate source figures against company reports before drawing conclusions.
